# Platform Style Patterns

Use these patterns as editing heuristics, not as instructions to copy another creator's video, audio, captions, thumbnails, or effects exactly.

## Cross-Platform Observations

- YouTube montages usually tolerate longer setup, clearer storytelling, widescreen framing, stronger color work, and fuller BGM arcs.
- Bilibili FPS edits often value readable kill confirmation, fandom vocabulary, dramatic BGM drops, and visible proof that a clip is a real high-kill moment.
- Douyin/TikTok edits reward fast hooks, vertical reframing, short loops, punchy captions, and immediate payoffs.
- Current short-video style does not require stern battle music. Pop rap, hyperpop, future bass, phonk, jersey/club, K-pop/J-pop hooks, anime-edit tracks, and upbeat electronic pop can work well if the kill proof remains readable.

## FPS Montage Grammar

- Cut on shots, reload snaps, weapon swaps, ability pops, dash/teleport motion, round-win banners, or beat transients.
- Use the game's own UI as proof: kill feed, skull icon, round win, ace/four-kill banner, score changes, and audible announcer calls.
- Avoid hiding crosshair movement during skillful moments; viewers want to see the aim correction.
- Use speed ramps before or after action, not during the exact headshot or final confirmation.
- Keep effects short: 2-6 frames for impact flash, 4-8 frames for shake, 8-18 frames for transition cards.
- Prefer hard cuts, whip cuts, dip-to-black, dip-to-white, match cuts, or cloned-frame freezes over long crossfades.

## Segment Template

1. Pre-roll: 0.25-1.20 seconds to establish angle, enemy pressure, or utility.
2. Kill chain: normal speed through the decisive kills.
3. Confirmation: hold until the kill icon/banner/round outcome is readable.
4. Freeze beat: 0.35-1.20 seconds cloned frame after final confirmation.
5. Transition: 0.12-0.35 seconds, after the freeze beat.
6. Next clip: start on readable map/weapon/context, then accelerate into action.

## Effect Palette

- Impact flash: brief exposure or white flash on confirmed kill.
- Micro shake: very short amplitude; do not smear UI proof.
- Chromatic aberration: only on transition or freeze, not during aim.
- Vignette: subtle on freeze moments.
- Sound hits: layer low hit, click, riser, or whoosh under the original game audio.
- Time stop: clone final confirmation frame, add small zoom, dim background, preserve banner.
- Beat cuts: align final kill, freeze, or transition card to downbeats.

## BGM Selection

- Prefer a track with a memorable hook, clear beat grid, and short repeatable chorus/drop.
- For playful edits, use brighter pop/electronic tracks instead of cinematic combat music.
- If using a copyrighted popular song, treat it as a private validation draft unless the user has rights or the platform provides the song in its music library.
- For publishable versions, use licensed files, platform-cleared audio, NCS-style tracks with required attribution, or other royalty-free libraries.
- Do not let loud vocals hide game hit confirmations; duck music slightly around dense kill chains if needed.

## Platform Variants

YouTube / Bilibili horizontal:
- Keep 16:9 framing.
- Allow 60-180 second runtime if material is strong.
- Use a complete BGM arc: intro, build, drop, second drop, outro.
- Keep subtitles minimal unless explaining context.

Douyin / TikTok vertical:
- Reframe to 9:16 with crosshair, target, and kill feed inside safe area.
- Open with the strongest kill in the first 1-2 seconds.
- Favor 12-45 second versions.
- Use captions only for labels, reactions, rank, agent, or punchline; do not cover crosshair or kill feed.

## Anti-Patterns

- Do not transition before the final kill confirmation appears.
- Do not cover kill banners with flash, zoom blur, captions, or logo overlays.
- Do not slow the whole montage; reserve slow motion for setup or post-confirmation.
- Do not include three-kill filler in a strict 4k/5k edit.
- Do not make the BGM louder than important game hit sounds unless the user asks for music-first style.
